METHODOLOGY

How the model works

A plain-English view of the pipeline. The goal is disciplined projection, not false precision.

01

Build team strength

Start with team efficiency, including EPA, success rate, explosiveness, havoc, scoring and drive-level production. Recent games receive more relevance than older ones.

02

Adjust for opponents

Raw production is adjusted for schedule quality and opponent strength so the same stat line is not treated equally against very different competition.

03

Project the matchup

Offense and defense are paired with pace, drive volume and matchup style to estimate each team’s points, the home spread and the game total.

04

Add game context

Availability, depth, weather, travel, rest and venue context are audited separately. Material uncertainty can keep a projection in review even when the statistical model is complete.

05

Compare with market

The model projection is compared with a market reference. The difference is a research gap—not automatically a bet, edge, or expected profit.

06

Validate prospectively

Spread and totals architectures must earn promotion through out-of-sample performance. A validation HOLD cannot be overridden by one large model/market discrepancy.

Important limitations

College football changes quickly. Injury reporting can be incomplete, depth roles can shift, market prices move, and historical relationships can fail out of sample. Model probabilities are estimates—not certainties. Current validation states are displayed prominently because calibration matters as much as raw projection error.

HOW TO READ IT

Glossary

Quick definitions for first-time visitors.

Spread

A handicap expressed in points. A favorite has a negative spread; an underdog has a positive spread.

ATS

Against the spread. Grades whether a projected side covered the market spread used for comparison.

Moneyline

A price on which team wins outright. This site converts model win probability into a display-only implied American moneyline.

Total

The combined points line for both teams. The model total is the projected combined score.

Push

A tie against the spread or total. Pushes are separated from wins and losses.

Win probability

The model’s estimated chance that a team wins the game outright.

Model/market gap

The numerical difference between the model projection and a market reference. It is a research signal, not a wager instruction.

Data quality

A completeness/readiness measure for required model inputs. High input coverage does not guarantee predictive accuracy.
