Important distinction

This page explains the public process, not hidden coefficients or a universal formula. Exact model architecture differs by league. A data source or contextual factor can be collected and displayed without receiving canonical numerical influence.

Four layers. One clear boundary.

Every piece of information belongs to a role. The strongest safeguard in the project is keeping those roles separate instead of letting useful context silently become model logic.

01 · Canonical

Production projection model

The only layer allowed to create the official frozen projection for its league. CFB and NFL use separate architectures and coefficients.

02 · Challenger

Research candidates

Alternative models, features, adjustments, or thresholds can be tested prospectively. They do not inherit production status automatically.

03 · Benchmark

Markets + outside models

Sportsbooks, external ratings, prediction markets, trends, and similar sources provide comparison evidence unless explicitly validated and promoted.

04 · Context

Pregame information

Injuries, quarterback status, availability, weather, venue, travel, rest, coaching, and other matchup information are timestamped and audited separately.

Collect broadly. Promote narrowly.

The project can monitor far more information than the canonical models are allowed to use. That is intentional.

From pregame state to frozen projection.

The weekly workflow is built around preserving what existed before kickoff, not reconstructing a cleaner story after the result.

  1. 01

    Assemble the league's approved input state

    Only the inputs already authorized for that league's canonical architecture are allowed to drive the production calculation.

  2. 02

    Generate the independent projection

    The model produces projected team points, a home-team spread, game total, and straight-up win probabilities. Those outputs originate from the model, not from the sportsbook line.

  3. 03

    Freeze the projection and provenance

    The project preserves the model version, projection timestamp, snapshot or source reference, and the number that existed before the game.

  4. 04

    Attach market evidence separately

    Current market references can be compared with the model, and the publication-time market snapshot is saved for grading. Market information does not silently rewrite the frozen canonical projection.

  5. 05

    Audit context and readiness

    Pregame availability, quarterback state, weather, venue, referee information, data quality, reliability, and unresolved blockers remain visible so uncertainty is not hidden.

  6. 06

    Apply the publication / validation state

    A large model/market difference does not override a HOLD. Research output, recommendation status, and canonical status remain separate.

  7. 07

    Grade only after Final

    Once a game is final, the frozen projection is graded against the saved market benchmark. The final score never changes the pregame projection being evaluated.

Difference is not the same as edge.

The public board intentionally separates model output, market reference, model/market distance, win probability, and readiness metadata.

Model spread

Home-team perspective

modelSpreadHome is expressed from the home team's perspective. A negative value means the model makes the home team the favorite; a positive value means it makes the away team the favorite.

Spread difference

Market minus model

marketSpreadHome − modelSpreadHome. A positive value points toward the home side relative to market; a negative value points toward the away side. The magnitude is the distance between the two numbers.

Total difference

Model minus market

modelTotal − marketTotal. Positive means the model total is higher than market; negative means it is lower.

Win probability

Straight-up outcome

Home and away win probabilities describe the model's projected chance to win the game outright. They are not ATS cover probabilities.

Confidence / reliability

Readiness metadata, not certainty

Data quality, signal strength, spread/total reliability, release status, and blockers describe the state of the research output. They should not be read as a guaranteed probability of covering a spread.

“Edge” language

Use with care

When older project language uses “edge,” treat it as a research model/market difference unless a separately validated expected-value process is explicitly identified. The current public UX prefers “difference.”

Illustrative example

No current game implied

If the model makes the home team -6.0 and the saved market is -3.5, the spread difference is +2.5 toward the home side. If the model total is 48.0 and market is 45.5, the total difference is +2.5 toward Over. Those differences still do not override a validation HOLD or become official wager recommendations by themselves.

Three grades. One preserved benchmark.

The record tracker measures the projection positions that actually existed. It is not a bet slip and it does not choose a better line after the result.

ATS

Against the spread

The model's side is inferred from the frozen model spread versus the saved publication-time market spread. That exact saved spread is the benchmark used for Win, Loss, or Push.

Totals

Over / Under

The model total is compared with the saved publication-time market total. The final combined score is graded against that preserved number, including pushes.

SU

Straight up

The projected outright winner receives a Win or Loss based only on the final winner. Saved moneyline data is reference metadata and does not change the SU grade.

Grading safeguards

  • Games remain pending until the result is Final.
  • Missing or invalid publication-time market locks are not silently reconstructed after the game; they can remain excluded or under review.
  • Poor weeks remain visible in the cumulative record.
  • Canonical and challenger results stay separate unless a challenger is explicitly promoted.
  • Market movement after publication does not rewrite the benchmark used for the published projection grade.

A useful idea is not automatically a model feature.

Promotion is deliberately harder than collection. That keeps the project from overfitting one bad week, one strong week, or a compelling narrative.

1

Hypothesis

Identify a candidate feature, adjustment, model architecture, or rule.

2

Leakage-safe history

Test only information that would genuinely have been knowable before kickoff.

3

OOS / walk-forward

Require evidence outside the sample used to form or tune the idea.

4

Prospective frozen weeks

Track the candidate before results with timestamps and frozen predictions.

5

Explicit promotion

Only after evidence is strong enough can the candidate receive canonical influence.

NON-NEGOTIABLE

The project preserves evidence even when the model is wrong.

No postgame reconstruction of missing pregame information. No retroactive coefficient changes to make a frozen week look better. No external benchmark silently becoming canonical. No challenger promoted because it happened to win one slate. The purpose of the record is to make improvement measurable, including when the result is poor.
